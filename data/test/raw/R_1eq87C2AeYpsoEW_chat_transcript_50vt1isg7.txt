**Chapter 13 Bankruptcy:**
- Often called a "reorganization" or "wage-earner plan."
- Designed for individuals with a regular income who want to keep their assets.
- Allows you to create a court-approved repayment plan to pay off debts over 3 to 5 years.
- You may be able to catch up on missed mortgage or car payments through the plan.
- Debts are not automatically discharged; you pay them off over time.
- Typically involves more ongoing court supervision and a lengthier process compared to Chapter 7.
- Suitable if you have significant assets you'd like to protect or if your income was too high for Chapter 7 qualification.

assistant: The income threshold for qualifying for Chapter 7 bankruptcy is determined by the **means test**. This test compares your household income to the median income in your state for a household of your size.

**If your income is below the median**, you're generally eligible for Chapter 7.  
**If your income is above the median**, you may not qualify for Chapter 7 unless you pass additional parts of the means test, which assess your expenses and ability to repay debts.

The means test looks at your income, expenses, and certain allowances to determine whether you have the means to repay your debts through Chapter 13 instead.
